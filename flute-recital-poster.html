Fantaisie brillante sur 'Carmen'

François Borne
The composer

François Borne was a French flutist and teacher associated with Toulouse, and a representative of the virtuoso French flute tradition of the late nineteenth century.

Historical background

Bizet's opera Carmen premiered at the Opéra-Comique in Paris on 3 March 1875; Bizet died only a few months later. Its melodies, including the Habanera, quickly became famous well beyond the theatre. Around 1900 Borne wrote this fantasy, in the popular tradition of opera paraphrases, to showcase the flute with piano accompaniment.

Musical character

Borne takes tunes from the opera and surrounds them with brilliant variations and ornament. Expect fast scales, rapid tonguing and wide leaps alongside songlike phrases, evoking Spanish rhythm, colour and theatrical flair. It has become one of the best-known showpieces of the flute repertoire.

Fantaisie, Op. 79

Gabriel Fauré
The composer

Gabriel Fauré, born in Pamiers, studied at the École Niedermeyer in Paris, where Camille Saint-Saëns was among his teachers. He became organist at the Madeleine, composed the Requiem (1888), and was director of the Paris Conservatoire from 1905 to 1920.

Historical background

Fauré wrote the Fantaisie in 1898 as the flute competition piece for the Paris Conservatoire, and dedicated it to Paul Taffanel, the flutist and professor who shaped the modern French flute school. Competition pieces of this kind were meant to test both musicianship and technique.

Musical character

A calm, singing opening displays tone, breath control and long melodic lines. A quick, sparkling section follows, demanding agility and clarity. Throughout, the piano part is transparent and the harmony subtly shifting, which gives the work its characteristic elegance and restraint.

Pièce

Jacques Ibert
The composer

Jacques Ibert, born in Paris, studied at the Paris Conservatoire, with Gabriel Fauré among his teachers. He won the Prix de Rome in 1919 and directed the Académie de France in Rome (Villa Medici) from 1937 to 1960. He is known for witty, colourful works such as Escales and his Flute Concerto.

Historical background

Ibert wrote Pièce for solo flute in 1936. It began as incidental music for the play Le Jardin du Paradis, and has since lived on as an independent concert piece for unaccompanied flute.

Musical character

With no accompaniment, the flute must carry everything alone. The music moves freely between dreamy lyricism and playful, agile passages, with wide contrasts of dynamics, register and tone colour. Ibert's wit and clarity give it an improvised, almost spoken character.
